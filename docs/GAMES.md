# Games: how the perfect-season simulator works

Simulated seasons are generated by the GMmode simulator. They are **not real games**. The rosters, player stats, fantasy points, schedules, opponent strength and chemistry links are real; the results are not.

Roster strength is **real fantasy production**. Each drafted player brings his fantasy points per game (PPG) from the season he was drafted from. Combined PPG is the sum of those PPGs plus verified chemistry bonuses. That total is placed among comparable rosters from the same sport's history, and the resulting percentile drives the simulation. There are no player ratings.

## 0. The draft (`lib/games/service.js`)

> Draft one player from each randomly generated historical team and season. After every selection, a new team and season are rolled automatically. You get one manual reroll per simulation.

1. **Start.** The server rolls a random team-season: a random covered season, then a random team from that season's real standings. The player sees that team's real roster.
2. **Pick.** The player drafts ONE player from that roster into an open slot (the one he targets, or else the first open slot he fits, specific slots before Flex/Bench/DH).
3. **Automatic roll.** As soon as the pick is saved, the server rolls a brand-new random team-season and loads its real roster. The next pick can only come from that roster.
4. Repeat until every slot is filled, then Review → Lock in → Simulate.

**What a roll can be.** Only team-seasons with complete data (no failed downloads, box scores for at least 80% of games), never one already rolled in this draft, and only rosters with at least one player who fits a slot still open and isn't already drafted. A roll that doesn't qualify is skipped and another is drawn (up to 8 tries). If ESPN can't deliver one, nothing changes: no roll is recorded and no reroll is spent.

**The one manual reroll.** Each draft gets exactly one. The opening roll, the automatic rolls after picks, and retries of a failed automatic roll never use it. Pressing it replaces the current team AND season with a new random roll, keeps every pick, and doesn't reset anything. After that, `rerollUsed` is stored on the challenge, so the button stays "Reroll used" for the rest of the draft, after more picks, after a refresh and on another device. A reroll that fails (ESPN down) keeps both the current team and the reroll.

**Server-side state.** The challenge record (`data/games.json`) holds: `picks` (in order: slot, player id, the team-season he came from, and a snapshot of his real stats, PPG and career facts as drafted), `roll` (the current team-season and its number `n`), `rollCount`, `rolled` (every team-season rolled in this draft), `rerollUsed`, `home` (the team-season of the first pick, whose schedule is played), `locked`, `runs` and `draft` (a counter for rebuilds). Refreshing or navigating away loses nothing.

**Validation.** Every pick names the roll it was made against (`roll: n`). The server checks: it's your challenge, the draft isn't over, `n` is the current roll, the player is on that roll's real roster, the same athlete isn't already in the squad (even from another season), and the slot is open and fits him. Picks, rolls and rerolls on one challenge are processed one at a time, so a double click or two tabs produce exactly one pick and one new roll; the other request gets a 409 and the current state.

**Picks are final.** There's no removing a pick mid-draft (that would be an unlimited reroll). After locking, **Rebuild squad** starts a fresh draft for the challenge (new rolls, a new reroll); earlier runs keep their own squads and replay exactly.

**API.** `POST /api/games/attempts {sport}` starts a draft. `POST .../attempts/:id/pick {playerId, roll, slot?}` drafts one player. `POST .../reroll {roll}` uses the reroll. `POST .../roll` retries a failed automatic roll. `POST .../lock`, `.../simulate`, `.../rebuild`. `GET .../attempts/:id` (the full draft state) and `GET .../runs/:n`. The old free-form `POST .../squad` endpoint is gone. Opening a challenge (`GET .../attempts/:id`) is also where an owner's unfinished draft without a current roll gets one: a failed automatic roll is retried once (never the reroll).

**Challenges saved by the single-roster version** are read through a compatibility view and never rewritten just by opening them. Locked, completed and simulated ones keep their old format, their runs and their replays (simulating one again adds a run). An **unfinished** one is upgraded once, when its owner opens it, under the same challenge id:
- its picks keep their original players, snapshotted from their original roster, which also stays the schedule;
- it gets a real random roll for the next pick and one unused manual reroll.

After that it's a normal draft, so reopening or refreshing changes nothing. If the original roster can't load, nothing is written and the next open tries again.

**Schedule.** A squad drafted from many seasons plays the real schedule of the team-season its **first pick** came from: those real opponents, in that real order, with that season's rules and length. Results compare the squad with how that real team did on the same schedule.

## 1. Data (`lib/games/history.js`)

Everything comes from ESPN's public API, the same free, keyless feed the scoreboard uses. No paid provider is involved.

| Step | Source | What we keep |
|---|---|---|
| Standings for the season | `apis/v2/sports/<sport>/standings?season=Y` | every team that played, its real record, points for and against |
| Team schedule | `teams/<id>/schedule?season=Y` | completed regular-season games in order, with real scores |
| Box score per game | `summary?event=<id>` | per-player lines for **this team only**, plus the team D/ST for NFL |
| NFL positions and college | `sports.core.api.espn.com/.../seasons/Y/athletes/<id>` | the season-scoped position and college |
| College (other sports) | `sports.core.api.espn.com/.../athletes/<id>` | the college id, named from `/v2/colleges/<id>` |
| Career history | `.../athletes/<id>/statisticslog` (soccer: `leagues/all`) | every franchise and season the player appeared for |
| National teams (soccer) | `sports.core.api.espn.com/v2/sports/soccer/teams/<id>` | ESPN's `isNational` flag and the team name |
| Team names in links | `.../seasons/Y/teams/<id>` | the name that season ("2005 Seattle SuperSonics") |

- A player enters the pool only if he appears in that team's box scores that season. His stats are summed from those games, so a traded player counts only his games with this team.
- Coverage (checked Nov 2026): NFL 2002 to 2025, NBA 1995-96 to 2025-26, NHL 1995-96 to 2025-26 (not the cancelled 2004-05), MLB 2005 to 2025, Premier League 2003-04 to 2025-26. Older seasons exist on ESPN without player lines and are left out.
- Data quirks are handled, not papered over:
  - A team can't meet the same opponent twice on one day (except an MLB doubleheader), so when ESPN lists a game twice, only the copy whose box score credits players is kept. This removes 0-0 "final" stubs (1999-00 Nets) and second copies with a wrong venue and score (every Fulham 2009-10 match).
  - A 0-0 "final" whose box score has no player lines is a placeholder and is dropped, even when it sits on a different day from the real game.
  - A box score only counts toward coverage if it credits at least one player to the team.
  - Postponed games are excluded. When ESPN's standings don't add up to the games played, the record is recounted from the real scores.
  - Soccer goals-against come from the real final score, because the per-player GA field is wrong in older feeds.
  - NHL shots come from the `S` column; ESPN's `SOG` column there is shootout goals.
  - NFL defensive touchdowns come from the `defensive` block only, because it already includes interception returns; counting the `interceptions` block too would double-count every pick-six.
- Where ESPN is simply missing games, the season plays the games it has and says so ("Real roster: N players from X of Y box scores"). Example: ESPN has 80 of the 1999-00 Nets' 82 games.
- **Missing is never zero.** A player's PPG divides by the games he appears in, so a missing box score shortens his sample rather than adding a zero game. A player with no games in a role has no PPG in that role (not 0).
- **Failed fetches are never cached.** ESPN requests retry three times with backoff (honouring 429 `Retry-After`). A 404 means ESPN has nothing, and that item is left out (for a career log: "history unknown", never invented). Any other failure (timeout, rate limit, 5xx), for a box score, a position, a college or a career log, marks the season `partial`. A partial season is never written to the cache, can't be rolled, and an existing challenge on it shows "try again in a minute" instead of a short roster. The next request re-fetches.
- A team-season with box scores for fewer than 80% of its games, or no player who fits a slot still open, is never offered as a roll (section 0).
- **No local data is needed.** A fresh install with an empty `DATA_DIR` fetches each team-season on first use (about 2 to 6 seconds, including career histories) and caches it in `DATA_DIR/games-history/`. The cache is an optimization only, and deleting it is safe. Fantasy points and chemistry are computed on read, so formula changes don't need a re-download. Bump `SCHEMA` (or a sport's entry in `SPORT_SCHEMA`) in `history.js` if the cached shape changes; older files are then ignored and re-fetched.
- The only data committed to the repo is `lib/games/norms.json` (section 4), which is derived from real seasons by `npm run calibrate-games`.

## 2. Fantasy PPG (`lib/games/fantasy.js`)

Each sport has one fixed formula, applied to a player's stats from **the season being played** (never career or current numbers). PPG = fantasy points ÷ games he actually played in that role (games with a box-score line; an MLB starter's PPG is per start, a reliever's per appearance). Only categories ESPN recorded in **every** covered season of the sport are scored, so a 1996 season and a 2024 season are scored the same way.

| Sport | Formula |
|---|---|
| NFL players (full PPR) | pass yd 0.04 · pass TD 4 · INT thrown −2 · rush yd 0.1 · rush TD 6 · reception 1 · rec yd 0.1 · rec TD 6 · fumble lost −2 · FG made 3 · FG missed −1 · XP made 1 · XP missed −1 |
| NFL D/ST | sack 1 · interception 2 · fumble recovered 2 · defensive or return TD 6 · points allowed: 0 → +10, 1-6 → +7, 7-13 → +4, 14-20 → +1, 21-27 → 0, 28-34 → −1, 35+ → −4 |
| NBA | point 1 · three made +0.5 · rebound 1.25 · assist 1.5 · steal 2 · block 2 · turnover −0.5 · double-double +1.5 · triple-double +3 |
| MLB hitters | single 1 · double 2 · triple 3 · HR 4 · run 1 · RBI 1 · walk 1 · stolen base 1 · strikeout −1 |
| MLB pitchers | inning 3 (1 per out) · hit −1 · earned run −2 · walk −1 · strikeout 1 · win 2 · loss −2 · save 5 · hold 2 |
| NHL skaters | goal 3 · assist 2 · plus-minus 1 · shot on goal 0.4 |
| NHL goalies | win 3 · save 0.2 · goal against −1 · shutout 3 |
| Soccer (by position) | start 2 · sub appearance 1 · goal GK/DEF 6, MID 5, FWD 4 · assist 3 · shot on target 0.5 · clean sheet as a starter GK/DEF 4, MID 1 · goal conceded as a starter GK/DEF −0.5 · save (GK) 1/3 · yellow −1 · red −3 · own goal −2 |

Where categories come from, and what's left out:

- **NFL.** Team defense and special teams are one D/ST unit: the rolled team's real defense, scored game by game from the same box scores (our sacks and interceptions, the opponent's lost fumbles, defensive and return TDs) and the real points allowed. Individual defenders aren't drafted, because box-score tackles reward bad defenses (they're on the field longer). Return TDs count for the D/ST, not the returner, so they aren't counted twice. Not scored: two-point conversions, field-goal distance, return yards, safeties, blocked kicks (not in every season's box score).
- **NBA.** Every category is in every season from 1995-96 on. Plus-minus isn't scored: ESPN leaves it blank before 2008-09.
- **MLB.** Doubles, triples and steals aren't box-score columns; they come from the box-score notes ("D Jeter 2 (10, ...)"), matched to a batter in that game by name. ESPN writes the shortest name that's unique in the game ("Ra Johnson"), so a name matches exactly one batter or none. Unmatched notes are counted, never guessed (2022 Dodgers: 14 of 454). Pitcher decisions (W, L, S, H) come from the pitcher's decision note. Not scored: hit-by-pitch, caught stealing, quality starts.
- **NHL.** Goalie wins and shutouts aren't box-score columns; they come from the real final score: the goalie with the most ice time on a winning night gets the win (the official rule credits the goalie in net for the winning goal, nearly always the same one), and a shutout needs one goalie for the whole game. Not scored: power-play points (ESPN only records goal strength from 2003-04 on), blocks and hits (blank before the mid-2000s).
- **Soccer.** Tackles, interceptions and minutes aren't in the feed for these seasons, so defenders score through clean sheets, goals conceded, goals and assists. Clean sheets count for starters. A player scores by the position of the slot he fills (a defender's goal is worth more), using the positions he actually started that season.

**Small samples.** A player's real PPG is used exactly as it is once he has a minimum sample: NFL 4 games, NBA 10, MLB 20 games (hitters), 5 starts, 10 relief appearances, NHL 10 games (skaters), 8 (goalies), soccer 5 appearances. Below that, his sample is topped up with replacement-level games up to the minimum, so two hot games can't pose as a season. Replacement level is the 20th percentile of real PPG at that position among regulars (players in at least 30% of their team's games), from the calibration sample. The draft board always shows the real PPG, and marks a topped-up player with "≈" and the number the sim uses.

## 3. Combined PPG and chemistry (`lib/games/chemistry.js`)

**Combined PPG = Σ each pick's PPG in the slot he fills + Σ chemistry bonuses.** No ratings or multipliers sit on top.

Chemistry comes only from ESPN records:

| Connection | Verified by | Bonus (share of each player's PPG) |
|---|---|---|
| Same college | both ESPN athlete records list the same college id | +1% |
| Former teammates | both appeared for the same franchise in the same season, up to the season each was drafted from (ESPN statistics log, plus the drafted team-season itself). Players drafted from different years of one franchise count only if their careers there overlapped. Same franchise in different years doesn't count. | +0.5% per shared season, up to 3 seasons (+1.5%) |
| National team (soccer) | both appeared for the same national team in the same international competition season (World Cup, Euros, qualifiers, friendlies, Copa América...), on a team ESPN flags `isNational` | +1% |

- Categories stack for a pair, up to **+2%**. A player's total from all his connections is capped at **+3%** of his PPG.
- No double counting: each pair once, each shared season once (two franchises in one season = one season), each national team once.
- A player's bonus is in fantasy PPG: his rate × his PPG. The team's chemistry PPG is the sum. Each link's share of that is shown in the draft and review.
- Sizing: from the simulations in section 6, a fully connected core gains a few wins a season at most (1995-96 Bulls about +4, 2015-16 Warriors about +5, most squads well under 1), far less than the gap between a strong and a weak draft. The first version used rates about 2.5× higher; the Bulls gained 14 wins from chemistry alone, so it was cut.
- College data: ESPN has colleges for most NFL and NBA players, many MLB players, few NHL players, and none for soccer. Career logs exist for all five sports (back to the 1980s for veterans). A player whose log is missing is shown as "history unknown"; he simply has no links.

## 4. Normalization (`lib/games/norms.js`, `lib/games/norms.json`)

Raw PPG is never compared across sports (or with a fixed target). For each sport, `npm run calibrate-games` builds a comparison distribution from a seeded sample of real team-seasons (NFL 30, NBA 28, MLB 18, NHL 28, soccer 28):

1. **Comparable rosters.** For each sampled team-season: 120 legal rosters with the challenge's exact slots, each slot filled at random in proportion to how many games each eligible player actually played, plus the team's best roster. 2,000-3,600 rosters per sport, combined PPG without chemistry.
2. **Era adjustment.** Rosters' fantasy output is regressed on the league's real scoring level that season (log-log). The slope α (clamped to 0-1.5) scales a roster's PPG by (L_ref / L)^α, so a high-scoring era's PPG counts for a little less. Fitted: NFL 1.5 (at the clamp), NBA 0.63; MLB, NHL and soccer showed no era trend (α = 0).
3. **Percentile.** The era-adjusted combined PPG is located in that sport's distribution with a smoothed CDF (Gaussian kernel, Silverman bandwidth), converted to a normal score z. Beyond the 1st/99th percentile the scale continues in a straight line at the tail's slope, so great rosters keep separating. z is clamped to ±4 (about 1 in 30,000 comparable rosters), so even a roster beyond anything in history can lose.

**Squads from many seasons.** Each pick is era-adjusted by the scoring level of the season he was drafted from (his PPG and chemistry bonus × (L_ref / L_his season)^α), and the adjusted total is placed in the distribution. For a squad from one season this is exactly the same as before.

Roster size, positional requirements and scoring environments are handled by construction: each sport's distribution is built from rosters with that sport's slots and scoring, drawn from real seasons across its eras. Games played and sample sizes are handled per player (section 2).

The percentile, z and the win-model constants stay on the server. Players see their combined PPG, chemistry and a projected record, never a percentile, a threshold or a "PPG needed" number.

## 5. Win probabilities and one game (`lib/games/sim.js`)

1. **Strength** s = b · (z − z0), calibrated per sport (section 6).
2. **Expected scores** for each game on the real schedule:
   - `us   = L · e^(+s/2) · oppDefense · home · form`
   - `them = L · oppOffense · e^(−s/2) / home · form`
   - `L` is that season's real league scoring average. `oppOffense` and `oppDefense` are that opponent's real points for and against per game divided by `L`. `form` is small game-to-game noise.
   - MLB: the day's starter rotates through your four, so s is recomputed per game with that starter standing in for the whole rotation (an ace day is stronger than a fourth-starter day).
3. A score is drawn from a distribution shaped like the sport. NFL: Poisson touchdowns and field goals. NBA: normal, SD 11. NHL and soccer: Poisson goals. MLB: overdispersed Poisson runs. Win probability is never set directly; it comes from these score distributions, so upsets always happen.
4. Ties follow that season's rules. NFL overtime (rare ties remain). NBA overtime until decided. MLB extra innings. NHL: ties through 2003-04, OT losses from 1999-00, shootouts from 2005-06, 3-on-3 OT from 2015-16. Soccer: draws.
5. Schedules are the real ones: 16 NFL games before 2021 and 17 after, lockout and pandemic seasons at their real length, the real opponents in the real order, home and away as played.
6. **Player lines** come from each player's real per-game rates, scaled to how the game went and tied to the score (points add up to the team total; goals, touchdowns and runs are credited to players). Each line is scored with the same fantasy formula, which gives the results' simulated PPG, MVP, weakest link and best performances. The D/ST line scores the simulated points allowed.

Everything is seeded: a run is stored as its squad plus seed and replays exactly (`GET /api/games/attempts/:id/runs/:n`). Each new run gets a new seed, so "Run it back" plays a new season while old runs keep their result. If the engine changes later (`ENGINE` in `service.js`), a replay that no longer matches is flagged and the stored summary stays authoritative.

## 6. Calibration (`npm run calibrate-games`)

For each sport the script drafts each sampled team's best legal roster (highest PPG per slot). For each candidate b it finds the z0 that removes the average bias between those rosters' expected win% and their teams' real win%, then picks the b whose expected records spread out like real team **talent** (the real spread of win% minus binomial schedule luck). Nothing targets perfect seasons. Results (Nov 2026), RMSE of best-roster expected win% vs. the real team's win%:

| Sport | b | z0 | RMSE | Talent spread | Previous ratings model |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NFL | 0.400 | 1.46 | 15.7 pts | 13.6 | 12.7 |
| NBA | 0.125 | 2.03 | 20.4 pts | 14.4 | 4.8 |
| MLB | 0.100 | 2.15 | 3.8 pts | 3.5 | 3.3 |
| NHL | 0.250 | 2.34 | 7.4 pts | 7.7 | 5.0 |
| Soccer | 0.525 | 1.12 | 4.7 pts | 13.3 | 4.2 |

**Read this honestly.** Fantasy production predicts real team records well in soccer, MLB and the NHL, whose formulas include result-linked categories (clean sheets, goals conceded, earned runs, pitcher and goalie wins, plus-minus). It predicts poorly in the NBA, and only moderately in the NFL even with the D/ST. Standard NBA fantasy scoring measures offensive volume and ignores points allowed, so a defense-first champion and a high-usage losing team can post similar totals. The model reflects the fantasy production you draft, as designed, which means it can disagree with history.

**NBA: a Team Defense unit was tested and not adopted (Nov 2026).** A D/ST-style NBA unit was tried. It scored points prevented per 100 possessions against the league that season, plus opponent turnovers that weren't our steals (steals and blocks already score for players). It was calibrated on the 28 production teams and judged on 28 other team-seasons. On that holdout, predicting .500 for every team scores RMSE 17.9; the current model scores 28.4, and Team D versions score 26.3 (light) to 24.5 (very heavy). Every version was worse than a constant guess. The bigger gains needed the unit to reach −11 to 48 PPG, more than any player, which just imports the real team's defensive rating. The underlying issue is that a team's best eight by fantasy PPG barely correlates with winning (about 0). The NBA challenge is therefore deliberately a pure fantasy-production game: results follow what you draft, not the real team's quality.

**With one pick per random team-season** (simulated drafts from cached real rosters, 30 per sport and strategy): a drafter who takes the best available player each pick builds squads stronger than most real teams. Win %: NFL 69%, NBA 92%, MLB 56%, NHL 66%, soccer 49%. Average perfect odds: NFL 0.9% (best draft 13%), NBA 0.5% (best 2%), MLB, NHL and soccer about 0. A drafter picking at random wins 15-39%. In the NBA (25 of 30 drafts), NHL (24/30) and MLB (15/30), most strong drafts reach the top of the strength scale (z = 4), where more PPG or chemistry stops helping. That is a known limitation of keeping the calibrated scale unchanged for this draft mode.

Stronger rosters always beat weaker ones on average, every team has a spread of outcomes, and dominant teams regress toward the middle (part of their real dominance came from things fantasy points don't measure, and from luck). Perfect seasons are rare and earned: a few percent for a 2007 Patriots roster, effectively zero for 82-0 or 162-0. Nothing was tuned to make them more common. Rosters at the top of the scale (2022 Dodgers, 2022-23 Bruins, 1995-96 Red Wings) gain nothing from more PPG or chemistry, by design of the ±4 cap.

## 7. Not modeled

Injuries, rest and in-season trades (your squad plays every game). Offensive linemen and baseball defense (box scores don't credit them). Coaching. Playoffs. The categories listed as not scored in section 2.
